# BTH Site Audit — Buyer Path

Date: 2026-05-03
Pages reviewed: 7 (index, about, addons, tier-1, tier-2, tier-3, reset)

---

## Direct Verdict

The brand is strong. The sales system is not fully ready yet.

Built to Hoop already has a real identity: adult hoopers, pickup-integrated training, readiness framework, pain/stiffness/rebuild angle, and a clear "train and play at the same time" promise. That is good. The homepage immediately says BTH is a "readiness-based training system built for adult hoopers" and gives the main CTA to the free reset/program.

But before you push serious traffic, you need to fix conversion leaks: the free reset email capture appears incomplete, one indexed page returns 404, Tier 2 has a CTA issue, trust proof is still thin, and the product ladder may feel like too many options for a cold buyer.

**Current score: 7/10 for brand direction. 5.8/10 for sales readiness.**

---

### 1. First impression

**What I think in the first 5 seconds**

The first impression is strong emotionally. "The Court Isn't Done With You" is a good BTH headline. It feels like a comeback brand, not a generic workout plan. The subheadline clearly says it is for adult hoopers who want to move better, train smarter, and stay on the court.

**Is the offer clear?**

Partially.

The free reset and Tier 1 are visible early. The homepage says Tier 1 is $19 one time and that the free reset requires no card. That is good.

The problem: there are too many directions too early: Free Reset, Tier 1, Tier 2, Tier 3, Add-Ons, Apparel/Early Access. A cold buyer may think, "Do I need the reset, Tier 1, Rise, Stay Ready, or an add-on?"

**Is the target customer clear?**

Yes. This is one of the strongest parts of the site. The homepage calls out adult hoopers, working schedules, stiffness, gym strength not transferring, sitting out from body issues, and players who are not chasing the pros but still want to play consistently.

**Does it feel premium, trustworthy, and athletic?**

Athletic: yes. Premium: mostly yes. Trustworthy: not fully yet.

It sounds real and specific, but it needs more proof: screenshots of the product, sample pages, testimonials, your own transformation, short video proof, training clips, or "inside the program" previews.

### 2. Brand clarity

**What is working**

Your brand idea is clear:

BTH = training system for hoopers who still play and need their body to hold up.

The "BTH Difference" section is very strong because it explains the unique mechanism: playing and training at the same time, using Light / Moderate / Hard readiness rules.

That is the best strategic asset on the site. That should be treated like the core invention of BTH.

**What needs work**

The site sometimes says too much before it proves enough.

Example: "nobody else in the market does this" is bold. It may be true from your perspective, but to a new buyer it can sound like a claim without proof. A stronger version:

> Most programs tell hoopers to train like basketball does not exist. BTH does the opposite — it adjusts training around how hard you actually played.

That feels more believable and still strong.

**Does the site explain why someone should trust BTH?**

Somewhat. The About page says BTH came from your own pain, training logs, injuries, comebacks, and helping friends. That is good founder credibility.

But the site needs more visible proof:

- "What's inside" screenshots
- sample weekly schedule
- sample exercise library page
- your own before/after story
- testimonials, even early beta ones
- short founder video
- visible product previews
- guarantee/refund clarity near every buy button

### 3. Offer analysis

**Tier 1**

Tier 1 is the cleanest offer. It is $19, one-time, instant Gumroad download, and built around a 3-day training structure with pickup recovery rules, progressive loading, hip/ankle reset, exercise library, and Keep the Bounce protocol.

This should be your main paid entry offer.

The value is clear enough, but it needs one thing: show me what I actually receive. Right now, the site tells me. It does not show me.

Add:

- 3 screenshots of the PDF/app/program
- "You get: PDF, exercise library, weekly schedule, pickup rules"
- sample week preview
- exact format: PDF? videos? Notion? email? Gumroad file? mobile-friendly?

**Tier 2**

Tier 2 has a stronger performance promise: 12 weeks, Strength to Bounce, Game Speed, Stay Ready. That is good.

But Tier 2 has two issues:

1. It may be too advanced for cold traffic.
2. The top CTA appears questionable. When I clicked the top "Get BTH Rise — $57" CTA, the page stayed on the Tier 2 page, while the bottom "Get Rise — $57" did resolve to Gumroad.

Also, the pricing line shows as "$67$57 launch price", which looks visually/technically messy.

**Tier 3**

Tier 3 is valuable, but it may be too much too soon. It includes Tier 1, Tier 2, all add-ons, monthly updates, Q&A, priority support, and annual/monthly pricing.

The issue is not the offer. The issue is buyer readiness.

A stranger probably should not see Tier 3 as equally important as Tier 1. Tier 3 needs to be framed as:

> Already bought in? Want the full BTH library and support? Join Stay Ready.

Right now it is listed beside Tier 1 and Tier 2 very early, which can create decision friction.

**Add-ons**

The add-ons are strong because they solve specific pain points: hip, knee, ankle, skill, recovery. The Add-Ons page explains each track well and gives specific bullets.

But add-ons should probably be secondary, not a major homepage focus before trust is built.

### 4. Sales page / conversion analysis

**Where the page is strong**

The homepage has a strong emotional hook, strong audience targeting, and a clear training philosophy. The sections "Who This Is For" and "Playing and Training at the Same Time" are very good.

**Where the page loses attention**

It starts strong, then becomes a catalog.

The buyer goes from:

1. Emotional hook
2. Problem identification
3. BTH method
4. Three tiers
5. Add-ons
6. Free reset
7. About
8. Apparel
9. Early access
10. Free reset again

That is too many directions for one homepage.

For sales, the homepage should push one main path:

> Free Reset → Tier 1 → Tier 3 later

Tier 2 and add-ons can still exist, but do not let them compete with the main funnel.

**Is the website built to sell or just look good?**

Right now, it is halfway.

It looks and sounds like a real brand, but the buying path is not sharp enough. A strong sales website makes the buyer think:

> "This is for me. I trust this. I know where to start. I know what I get. I know what to do next."

Your site already hits "this is for me." It needs more work on "I trust this" and "I know what to do next."

### 6. UX and layout analysis

**Navigation**

The nav is clean, but too many product choices show immediately: Tier 1 / Tier 2 Rise / Tier 3 Stay Ready / Add-Ons / About / Free Reset.

Better nav options:

- Start Here / Programs / Add-Ons / About / Free Reset
- Or simpler: Free Reset / Tier 1 / Full Library / About

**Buying path**

For a cold buyer, the site should say:

1. New here? Start free.
2. Ready to train? Buy Tier 1.
3. Want everything? Join Tier 3.

**Mobile-friendliness**

Based on content structure, the site is mobile-friendly in concept. The risk is that some headings may break awkwardly and the pricing issue "$67$57" may look messy on small screens.

### 7. Technical and bug check

**Serious issues to fix first**

**1. Free reset email capture may not be functional**

The Free Reset page says "Drop your email" and "Send Me Day 1," but the crawl did not show a form or input field.

This is the biggest conversion issue. If the freebie is the main funnel, the email capture must work perfectly.

Fix:
- Add visible email input
- Connect it to MailerLite/Gumroad/email automation
- Test opt-in on desktop and mobile
- Confirm Day 1 sends within 5 minutes
- Add success message
- Add backup "Email me if it doesn't arrive"

**2. Indexed /programs.html result returns 404**

Fix:
- Either rebuild `/programs.html`
- Or 301 redirect `/programs.html` to the homepage or Tier 1 page
- Then resubmit sitemap/indexing

**3. Tier 2 top CTA issue**

The top Tier 2 "Get BTH Rise — $57" did not appear to route to Gumroad, while the bottom CTA did.

Fix:
- Make every Tier 2 buy CTA point to the correct Gumroad URL
- Test top, middle, bottom buttons

**4. Pricing display issue**

Tier 2 price appears as "$67$57 launch price," which looks broken.

Fix:
- Format as: $57 launch price
- Smaller note: Regular price: $67 after first 5 testimonials

**5. Status inconsistency**

The About page says Tier 2 and Tier 3 are "launching to the founding group now," but the product pages say they are live.

Fix:
- Decide one truth: "Live now" / "Founding member launch" / "Coming soon"
- Use the same wording everywhere.

**Minor polish issues**

- "You feel stiffer than you used to.Getting loose…" needs a space after the period.
- Footer "Contact" appears as text, not clearly linked on some pages.
- Social handles slightly inconsistent: homepage shows TikTok `@builttohoop` and Instagram `@builttohoop2026`, while other footers show `@builttohoop`.

Trust elements to add first:

### 9. SEO and discoverability

**Current SEO strength**

The site naturally includes strong phrases: adult hoopers, basketball performance system, pickup-integrated training, vertical/bounce/performance, hip/knee/ankle rebuild, mobility, recovery, stay on the court.

**SEO weaknesses**

You need more pages that target specific search intent. Right now, most pages are product pages. SEO needs problem-solving pages.

**Suggested page titles**

- Homepage: `Built to Hoop | Basketball Training for Adult Hoopers Who Still Play`
- Tier 1: `BTH Foundation | 3-Day Basketball Training System Built Around Pickup`
- Free Reset: `Free 5-Day Basketball Reset | Hip, Ankle, Movement & Bounce Reset`

**Suggested meta descriptions**
