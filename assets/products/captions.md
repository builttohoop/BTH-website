## 012 — Hip Pain
**Slides:** `BTH_012_Slide_01.png` … `BTH_012_Slide_07.png`

### Instagram
You feel it before you even break a sweat — that deep ache in the hip on the first hard cut. So you stretch longer. Foam roll more. It loosens for an hour, then locks right back up by the next run.

Here's the part nobody tells you: tight isn't the problem. Your hips are absorbing load they were never built to carry, and stretching a hip that needs *strength* just resets the clock. Mobility without strength is a loop you never get out of.

The fix is an order, not a stretch: build strength through the range, wake the glutes up so the hip flexors stop taking the whole hit, and recover the hip like it matters — because it eats every cut and landing you make.

Ignore it and it climbs. Into your back. Your knees. Your first step. One day the hip isn't tight — it's done.

Your hips aren't old. They're underbuilt.

## 013 — Knee Pain
**Slides:** `BTH_013_Slide_01.png` … `BTH_013_Slide_07.png`

### Instagram
Down the stairs the morning after a hard run — that's not soreness. That's your knees having a conversation you keep ignoring. So you ice it, grab the ibuprofen, tell yourself it's just age, and go play on it again.

But healthy knees are stuck in the middle. When the joints above and below — your hips and ankles — stop doing their job, the knee absorbs the bill. It's paying for work the rest of the chain isn't doing.

You protect a knee by *building* it: bulletproof the tendons with slow, heavy, patient work, strengthen the hips and ankles around it, and teach it to land before you ask it to load. That's it. That's the whole game.

Keep ignoring it and you stay one bad landing from the sideline — never injured, never free either. That's no way to play.

Your knees aren't shot. They're unprotected.

## 014 — Ankle Pain
**Slides:** `BTH_014_Slide_01.png` … `BTH_014_Slide_07.png`

### Instagram
You roll it a little and hold your breath. You've started taping up before you even step on the court. And every season, your ankles feel a little less like yours.

You call them weak ankles — so you lace tighter, brace up, play careful. But here's the trap: a brace *holds* the ankle, it doesn't *build* it. Support from the outside lets the inside get weaker. The ankle never has to do its job, so it never gets strong enough to.

Rebuild it, don't wrap it: strength before support, train balance and reaction so your feet save you faster than tape ever could, and rebuild the foot too — because a strong ankle on a weak foot still folds.

Tape it, ignore it, repeat — and the ankle gets looser while the trust gets thinner. Until one cut takes the season.

Your ankles aren't weak. They're untrained.
