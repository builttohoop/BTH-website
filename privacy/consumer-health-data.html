Consumer Health Data Privacy Notice

BTH Return-to-Hoop Coaching  ·  Effective [EFFECTIVE DATE]

This notice covers the health-related information you share with us inside the Return-to-Hoop coaching program. We wrote it to the standard of Washington's My Health My Data Act (RCW 19.373) and we apply that standard to every client, in every state — not just Washington. It is short because our answer is simple: we collect only what coaching needs, we use it only to coach you, and we never sell it or hand it to advertisers.

1. Nothing is collected until you opt in

The application you fill out to apply for coaching asks no health questions — name, email, ZIP, goals, availability. That's it.

Health-related information is collected only after you become a client, and only after you give separate, written opt-in consent at intake. That consent names this data class specifically. If you don't consent, we don't collect it — and we'll talk through whether coaching can still work for you. You can withdraw that consent at any time (see Section 8).

2. What we collect

Once you've opted in, coaching involves these categories of consumer health data:

  • Readiness and comfort logs — the weekly check-in where you tell us how your body is responding to training and daily life.
  • Measurements — the numbers we take at Weeks 0, 4, 8, and 12 to track your progress.
  • Movement and basketball video — clips you send us and video from sessions, used for film review and programming.
  • Intake questionnaire answers — the PAR-Q+ and intake questions you answer when the program starts.
  • Clearance documentation — if screening calls for it, a note from your licensed healthcare provider confirming you're cleared to train.

Source: all of it comes from one place — you. We do not buy, scrape, or pull health information about you from anyone else.

3. Why we collect it

One purpose: to deliver your coaching. Programming, load decisions, film review, progress tracking, and knowing when to refer you out to a licensed healthcare provider. Nothing else.

4. What we never do with it

  • Never sold. Not to anyone, not in any form.
  • Never shared with advertisers or ad platforms.
  • Never placed in ad pixels, tracking URLs, analytics event names, or ad audiences. Our site tracking is built so health data structurally cannot reach it.
  • Never used to build advertising or marketing profiles.
  • Never used in marketing without a separate, written media release. Your service consent covers coaching only. If we ever want to use a clip of you publicly, that is a separate document you can freely decline, and declining changes nothing about your coaching.

6. Your video: no face recognition, no biometrics

We do not run face-recognition or biometric-template processing on your video. Ever. Your clips are watched by your coach for movement and basketball — that's the whole pipeline. Any future video-analysis tool gets vetted against this promise before it touches a single clip.

7. Calls

Some coaching and consult calls may be recorded so we can review them for your programming. We tell you at the start of any call that is being recorded — no call is recorded without you hearing that notice first. Recordings are handled under this notice like everything else here.

8. How long we keep it, and your rights

Retention: we keep your coaching records for [RETENTION WINDOW], then delete them. Video follows the same window unless you ask for earlier deletion.

At any time, you can:

  • See it — ask what we hold about you, and who it has been shared with (the Section 5 list).
  • Export it — get a copy of your logs, measurements, and video in a usable format.
  • Withdraw consent — stop future collection of any category, effective immediately.
  • Delete it — ask us to delete your consumer health data. We delete it from our systems and instruct our processors, including backups, to do the same.

How: email [PRIVACY EMAIL] from the address on your account. We respond within 45 days. There is no charge and no penalty — your coaching does not get worse because you exercised a right.

Appeals: if we decline a request, we'll tell you why in writing, and you can appeal by replying to that decision. If we deny your appeal, you may contact the Washington State Attorney General at atg.wa.gov, or your own state's attorney general.

9. Changes

If we change how we handle consumer health data, we update this notice, move the effective date, and tell active clients directly before the change applies to them.
