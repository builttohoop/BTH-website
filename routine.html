You already know something's off.

You get up out of bed and your body files a complaint before your feet hit the floor. You feel it again in the first ten minutes of a run. One side loads different. One knee talks to you on the way down. You've been stretching the thing that feels tight for two years and it keeps coming back.

Here's the part most guys never get told: the spot that hurts is usually not the spot causing it. For a lot of hoopers a stiff ankle turns up as a sore knee, and a hip that won't rotate turns up as a back that won't quiet down. You can stretch the loud one forever and never touch the quiet one.

Nobody is watching you move. That's the actual problem.

Why I built this

I got hurt bad enough that a clinic handed me pain pills and sent me home. Nobody trained me back.

So I learned it in order, on myself. I worked the back, and the knee got loud. I settled the knee down, and the arch started taking the load. Most programs train a body part. The problem was never a body part — it's a chain, and the weakest link goes first. That's why the order matters more than the exercise.

And nobody ever watched me move. Not once. I was guessing at my own body from the inside, which is the hardest place to see it from.

I'm still in it — the knee is the link I'm working on right now. I'm not selling you the end of this. I'm the first person it has to hold up for, and I'm still the one testing it.

So we watch.

That's what this is. You run a short movement check on your phone — no gym, no equipment, no coach standing over you. Five items, pass or fail, about five minutes. It tells you which ones came back clean and which didn't.

Then you film just the ones that didn't and send them straight back — reply to the same email I sent the check in, paste your copied results, attach the clips. That's the whole handoff. I watch your footage myself — not software, not somebody else. Then I write down what I saw.

What comes back is a night routine built for what the check and the clips showed. Not a stretching PDF with your name on the cover. Twelve to fifteen minutes, done at night, four weeks long. Then you run the same check again and we look at what moved.

What lands in your inbox

  • Three things I noticed. Plain language: what I saw your body do, where, and on which side. Observations, not a diagnosis.
  • Why those three connect. The chain, not a list. Ankle to knee to hip is one conversation, not three problems.
  • Your routine. One movement per page. Sets, holds, a cue, and the mistake most people make on it.
  • A four-week ladder. What changes in week two, three and four, so it progresses instead of going stale.
  • Your re-check. What to run again in four weeks, and what better would look like if you saw it.

Three questions first

The request button fills these in for you. If your mail app strips them, or you'd rather just type, answer them in your own words — they matter more than anything else on this page.

Before we start, three quick ones — yes or no:

  1. Pain that wakes you at night, or pain at rest?
  2. Any numbness, tingling, or a leg that gives way?
  3. Any joint that locks or catches mid-movement?

A yes to any of the three and I answer you personally instead of sending the check. Those three aren't movement findings. They're a reason to have somebody look at you in person, and no routine substitutes for that.

Questions

How long until I get mine?

The check goes out the same day you ask — that part is built and I'm not making you wait on it. The routine is the part I write by hand, so requests are worked in the order they come in. When yours is up you'll hear from me. I'm not going to give you a date I can't keep, because I'd rather keep the one I give you.

What do I need to run it?

A phone, a wall and a chair. Prop the phone on a shelf, a stack of books, whatever's there. No tripod, no gym, no partner. About five minutes.

Do I have to show my face?

No. I need to see how you move, not who you are. Your footage isn't shared with anyone unless you tell me it's okay — it stays in my email, and I don't upload it anywhere or run it through anything else.

Can I submit more than once?

Once every four weeks. That's not a restriction — four weeks is about the shortest window where the work produces a change worth measuring. Sooner and you'd just be filming noise.

What if I'm hurt right now?

Then this isn't the right thing yet. Pain that wakes you at night, numbness, a leg that gives way, a joint that locks — those need someone in the room with you, not video. Go see somebody. I'll be here after.

Is it really included?

Included with Stay Ready for the first group who ask, and when that group is full I'll say so right here. Stay Ready is $27/mo, month to month, cancel any time with one email — no refunds on a month already started. The routine doesn't cost extra right now. That won't be true forever.

Who's actually looking at my video?

Me. That's the whole point, and it's also why this can't go to everybody at once.

Does Foundation or Rise count?

Not for this one. Foundation and Rise are one-time programs. This is a Stay Ready thing — monthly or annual, either works.
