Best Basketball Warmup for Pickup.

If your warmup is jogging two laps and shooting around, you're not warming up. You're delaying the workout. Here's what an actual hooper warmup looks like.

What a Warmup Is For

A warmup has exactly two jobs:

  • Get your nervous system ready to produce force
  • Get your joints into the range of motion the game will demand

That's it. It's not for cardio. It's not for getting a sweat. It's not for skill work. If your warmup is doing those things and skipping the two real jobs, you're skipping the warmup.

The Truth
"A real warmup tells your body what's about to happen — so the first hard cut isn't the one that pulls something."

The 3 Mistakes Almost Every Hooper Makes

Mistake 1: Static stretching first. Holding a hamstring stretch for 30 seconds before you play actually decreases force production for the next 20 minutes. Save static stretching for after.

Mistake 2: Jumping straight to game-speed. Going from cold to first-step explosion is the fastest way to pull a hamstring. Build up.

Mistake 3: Skipping ankles and hips. These are the two joints that drive everything else in basketball. If they're cold, the knee picks up the slack. That's how knee pain starts.

The 8-Minute BTH Warmup

Before any pickup run, do this in order. The whole thing takes 8 minutes:

  • 90 seconds — slow jog or rope skip (just to get blood moving)
  • 2 minutes — ankle rocks (10 each foot) + hip CARs (5 each side)
  • 2 minutes — leg swings (front-back, side-to-side, 10 each)
  • 90 seconds — bodyweight squats slow, then 10 with a 1-second pause at the bottom
  • 1 minute — light line skips, A-skips, B-skips
  • 30 seconds — 3 broad jumps building to 80% effort

By the end of those 8 minutes you've moved through every range of motion the game demands and you've told your nervous system what's coming. Now you can play.

Why Most People Skip It Anyway

Because it feels uncool. Because guys around you aren't doing it. Because you're 32 and you remember when you didn't need it.

But here's the math: the warmup costs you 8 minutes. A pulled hamstring costs you 6 weeks. The day-after-pickup knee pain costs you the next decade of being able to play.

That's the trade. The hoopers who keep playing into their 40s are the ones who eventually stopped fighting this trade.
