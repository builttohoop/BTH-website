How to Train Legs for Basketball.

If your leg day leaves you slow and heavy on Tuesday's pickup run, you're training for a different sport. Here's what court-transfer leg training actually looks like.

The Mistake Most Hoopers Make

Generic gym programming optimizes for one thing: how much weight you can move. Basketball doesn't care how much weight you can move. Basketball cares how fast you can produce force, in a specific position, on one leg, after 90 minutes of running.

Those are different qualities. Train for one and the other doesn't show up automatically.

The Truth
"Court strength isn't gym strength. Your gym numbers go up and your bounce stays gone."

The 3 Qualities You Actually Need

1. Single-leg strength. Basketball is single-leg. Your training should be too — at least 60% of your leg volume. Step-ups, split squats, single-leg RDLs, walking lunges, single-leg presses.

2. Speed of force production. Strength matters. Strength expressed in 0.2 seconds matters more. Trap bar deadlifts, jump squats, contrast training, ballistics. The bar moves fast or it doesn't transfer.

3. Capacity in basketball ranges. The ranges you actually use on the court — deep hip flexion, full ankle dorsiflexion, knee out over the toe. Train through them, loaded. Goblet squats with pause, deficit RDLs, B-stance squats.

The Sample Week

Day 1 — Strength to Bounce. Trap bar deadlift, single-leg RDL, walking lunge, plyometric finisher (4 sets of 3 broad jumps).

Day 2 — Speed and Range. Goblet squat with pause, B-stance squat, hip thrust, lateral lunge, ankle work.

Day 3 — Movement Day (or pickup). If pickup, that's the work. If not, hill sprints, plyos, change of direction.

If Sunday is a pickup day, train Monday off legs, Tuesday lower volume, Wednesday rest. Don't lift heavy legs the day before pickup. Don't lift heavy legs the day after, either.
