How Many Days a Week Should You Play Pickup?

There's no universal number. The real question isn't how many days you play — it's whether your recovery can keep up with your volume. For a lot of grinders right now, it can't.

The Number Everyone Wants Doesn't Exist

Every hooper who plays 3-4x a week has asked this. Is it too much? Should I cut a day? The honest answer: there's no fixed number that's right for every body. Age, sleep, what else you're training, and — the one nobody accounts for — what you actually do between games all change the math.

Two guys can both play four nights a week. One's fine by Friday. The other's still limping into Sunday. Same volume, different outcome. So the number isn't the real variable.

The Real Question: Volume vs. Recovery Capacity

Pickup is high-impact and unstructured. No standard warmup, no cooldown, no mobility work built in — you show up, you run, you leave. If you're stacking that up three or four nights a week with nothing between games to help your body catch up, that's usually where the breakdown starts. Not the frequency by itself.

The volume you're already doing might be fine — if the recovery side of the equation exists at all. For most grinders, it currently doesn't.

Signs You've Crossed the Line

A few patterns tend to show up when volume has outrun recovery capacity:

  • Soreness that used to fade by Tuesday is still hanging around by the weekend.
  • Your warmup keeps getting longer just to feel like a normal human being.
  • Your first quarter is rough every single time out — not just on an off night.
  • Sleep and motivation are both sliding, not only your joints.

None of that is a diagnosis — it's a pattern worth paying attention to. If something feels off structurally, that's a conversation for a doctor or PT, not a blog post.

The 3-Input Formula That Actually Matters

What separates the guy who plays four nights a week and stays sharp from the guy who breaks down on the same schedule usually comes down to three things: how hard the games actually are, what recovery inputs happen between them, and the strength/mobility base he's carrying into the season. Games played is only one part of that equation — and probably the least controllable one.

The Truth
It's not how many days you play. It's what you do on the days you don't.

The Fix Isn't Playing Less

For a grinder who loves the game, "just play less" is advice that doesn't stick — and it usually isn't the actual fix. The move that tends to work better: build a real recovery system on the days between games, so the volume you're already putting in stops quietly grinding you down — that's what a recovery system built for it lets you do.

That's mobility work, tendon-health input, and a structure to your week — not just showing up and hoping Tuesday's legs are ready for Thursday's run.
