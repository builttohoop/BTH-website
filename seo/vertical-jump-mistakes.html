5 Vertical Jump Mistakes Hoopers Make.

Most vertical jump programs work for two months and then stop working. Here are the 5 mistakes that cause the plateau — and the fixes.

Mistake 1 — Doing 200 Jumps a Day

More volume isn't more progress. Force production is a quality, not a quantity. 200 jumps at 70% effort doesn't transfer — it just buries your legs in fatigue and limits the next session.

Fix: 15–25 high-quality jumps per session, full effort, full recovery between sets. Quality first, volume only after the quality is dialed.

Mistake 2 — No Strength Base

You can't express force you don't have. If you can't squat 1.5x your bodyweight, the ceiling on your vertical is going to plateau fast — because the engine is too small.

Fix: Build strength first. Trap bar deadlift, squat, single-leg RDLs. Get to a real strength base before chasing pure vertical work.

The Truth
"You can't out-jump a weak engine. Build strength, then convert it."

Mistake 3 — Skipping the Eccentric

Most vertical training is all concentric — the way up. But the way DOWN is where most jump height actually comes from. The faster and more controlled you can absorb the landing, the higher the next jump.

Fix: Add depth drops, controlled-tempo squats (3–4 seconds down, fast up), and reactive work. Train the eccentric like a separate quality.

Mistake 4 — Doing Plyos When Tired

Plyometrics are a nervous-system tool. If you do them at the end of a heavy lift, the nervous system is fried — you're training mediocre quality.

Fix: Plyometrics first or on their own day. Heavy lifting AFTER, not before.

Mistake 5 — Not Progressing the Stimulus

Bodyweight box jumps for 6 weeks isn't progressive overload — it's just maintenance. Once your nervous system adapts, the same input stops driving adaptation.

Fix: Progress the stimulus. Add weight (weighted jumps). Add complexity (depth jumps, reactive plyos). Add velocity (low boxes, fast contact). Same logic as strength training — the stimulus has to keep evolving.

The Right Sequence

Strength → Reactive Strength → Speed-Strength → Plyos → Sport.

If you skip a step, you plateau. If you do the steps in the wrong order, you plateau faster. Tier 2 Rise sequences these correctly across 12 weeks.
