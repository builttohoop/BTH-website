The Four-Week Block

One Job.
Four Weeks.

Rise · 4 Weeks
Strength to Bounce
Take the strength from Foundation and ask for elastic, springy output. A heavy lift, then a jump. Short sprints, never conditioning. Four days a week, with the two lower days kept apart so your legs come in fresh.
  • Smith squat, Romanian deadlift, Bulgarian split squat
  • Box jumps, broad jumps and pogos with clean landings
  • Short acceleration and build-up sprints
  • Calf and shin work
  • Chest, back, shoulders, arms and abs on the upper days
  • Week 4 is a primer: a lighter week so you finish fresh
Optional · 2 Weeks
Rise Tendon Bridge
Two lighter weeks between Rise and Dominate. Optional by design. Slow holds and controlled reps for quads, calves and soleus, single-leg control, and a low-contact landing day. Upper body and abs stay on the same path, with easy conditioning and a short nightly cooldown.
  • Four days a week, two weeks
  • Slow holds and controlled reps
  • Single-leg control, weaker side first
  • A low-contact landing day
  • Upper body and abs on the same path
  • Skip it and go straight to Dominate, or take it first

The Bridge is training, not treatment. If something is sharp, swollen or changing how you move, stop and get it checked.

What a Week Looks Like

Four Days.
Room to Recover.

Four training days, then rest: Day 1, Day 2, off, Day 3, Day 4, off, off. The lower days sit at least 48 hours apart, and if your legs are still heavy you add one more rest day.

Day 1

Lower A: Force + Jump Prep

Heavy lower-body strength with jump work. Sharp, not grinding.

Day 2

Chest + Arms + Abs

Upper size and core. Your legs get the day.

Day 3

Lower B: Single-Leg Power

One leg at a time, plus elastic work. Weaker side goes first.

Day 4

Back, Shoulders + Arms

Posture, shoulders, arms and an abs circuit.

Questions

Real Answers.

Do I need Foundation first?
Rise is open to all. If you are newer to loading, Foundation first. It's not a gate. It's a sequence: strength first, then bounce.
Why is it a launch price?
$97 one-time. That's it. No fake urgency. If you get in now, you lock in the rate and you own it forever.
Can I do this while still playing pickup?
Rise is four training days with rest between the lower days. Pickup counts as a leg day. Place the lower days away from your games, and use the extra rest day when your legs are heavy.
What equipment do I need?
A Smith machine or squat rack, dumbbells, a bench, a box, and room for short sprints.
What is the Bridge, and do I need it?
The Rise Tendon Bridge is an optional two-week block between Rise and Dominate. Take it if you want two lighter leg weeks first, or your lower-body work has started to feel flat or heavy. Skip it if you feel sharp. Dominate works either way.
What about the 12-week version?
Rise 12-Week is still here, and every Rise purchase includes it. The new Rise is one job in four weeks; the 12-week version runs three blocks back to back.
