The 12-Week System

Three Phases.
One Progression.

Phase 3 · Weeks 1–4
Strength to Bounce
Convert the base strength from Tier 1 into elastic, springy output. Slow strength work paired with jump intent. The bridge between weight room and court.
  • Heavy goblet + Bulgarian splits
  • Controlled box jumps with clean landings
  • Pogos, skips, tempo rebounds
  • Hip-dominant posterior work
  • Readiness-based loading
  • 2x strength + 1x movement per week
Phase 4 · Weeks 5–8
Game Speed
Cuts. Decelerations. Direction changes. The transfer phase. Train the exact movements that happen inside every possession — at the speeds they actually happen.
  • Reactive step + cut patterns
  • Scissor jumps, skater bounds
  • Single-leg stability under load
  • Defensive slide + recover work
  • Deceleration from full speed
  • Pickup-integrated programming
Phase 5 · Weeks 9–12
Stay Ready
Lock in what you built. Maintenance logic, game-day protocols, recovery integration. You stop dropping off after heavy weeks. You start staying ready — for the next run, the next season, the next decade.
  • Pre-game warm-up protocol
  • In-season minimum effective dose
  • Off-day recovery layer
  • Reactive peaking sets
  • Ongoing readiness framework
  • Graduation into Tier 3 if you want it
What a Week Looks Like

Built Around
Your Pickup.

Three intensity levels. Three training days. The readiness framework decides the rest — no guessing when to load and when to pull back.

High Day

Load + Intent

Max-intent strength work. Heaviest of the week. Placed on days with no pickup before or after.

Moderate Day

Strength + Bounce

Strength work with bounce intent. Lower volume than High Day. Lives near pickup, not on it.

Low Day

Movement + Reset

Skill work, movement patterns, prehab. The session that protects everything else you did.
